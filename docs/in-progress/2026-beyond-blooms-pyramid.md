abstract: >
  A conceptual paper arguing that generative AI changes how learners
  create, judge, explain, and show what they know, so grading only the
  AI-assisted deliverable hides what a student actually understands.
  Drawing on sociomateriality (Orlikowski), which treats learning
  practices as entanglements of people and technology, it keeps Bloom's
  Taxonomy as a reference point rather than declaring it obsolete. It
  presents CEAU (Create/Scaffold, Evaluate, Analyze, Understand &
  Refine) as a design case from AI 410: students may use AI to build a
  first working version, worth 20% of the grade, while the remaining
  80% assesses their critique, explanation, and ability to change and
  defend the work without AI. Two research questions ask how
  generative AI reconfigures human and material agency, and how CEAU
  turns sociomaterial principles into assessment of judgment and
  competence. An EMBA course serves as an illustrative transfer case.
  The paper reports no learner outcome data; it closes with
  propositions and a pilot protocol for a follow-on cohort study.
